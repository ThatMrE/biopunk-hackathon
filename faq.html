FAQ

Everything you're wondering.

Answers for the three specimens who show up: the builder who's never touched a pipette, the organizer who wants to run one, and the sponsor deciding whether to fund it.

// For builders
Do I need a background in biology or AI?

No. Curiosity > credentials. Biopunk is beginner-friendly by design — scientists, product designers, software engineers, and creatives all welcome. Mentors run intro workshops and a lab safety officer supervises the bench. If you're open-minded and hungry to build, you're already qualified.

Is it really free to enter?

Yes — no entry fee, ever. That's the whole contrast with iGEM, where teams pay roughly €7,700–€8,700 before anyone travels. At a Biopunk, the expensive part (reagents, DNA, sequencing, lab time) is covered by in-kind sponsors, and money flows toward participants as prizes.

Can my project be software-only?

Yes. Plenty of winning projects are software, AI, or design. But the thing that makes Biopunk different from every other hackathon is the wet lab — so if your idea can touch a bench, we'll help you get it there.

Do all teams get lab access?

The prototype weekend is open to everyone. Lab week is where selection kicks in: teams that pitch a feasible, safe, green-lit experiment advance to the bench with dedicated workspace, mentorship, and reagents. It's a two-phase build — not everyone advances, and that's the point.

What if my idea changes after the pitch?

Expected — ideas mutate. As long as the pivot stays inside the biosafety envelope (BSL-1, non-human, non-clinical) and your Lab & Safety Lead signs off, evolve away. That's science.

How big are teams?

Teams of 2–4 organisms. No lone wolves — packs can be formed onsite at the team-formation mixer. Mixed backgrounds (tech, bio, design, chaos) are encouraged.

What do I actually win?

Real Biopunk editions have handed out $15,000+ in cash and in-kind prizes — cash, a month of Biopunk Lab membership, Light.bio glowing petunias, Asimov Press books, discounted longevity-conference tickets, and mystery bio-loot. Plus the project itself, written up as a portfolio piece you keep.

Can I bring an existing project?

Biopunk favors building from scratch, in the moment — no recycled ideas. Bring your skills, your curiosity, and your own hardware/wetware if you want, but the build happens during the event.

What should I bring?

A laptop, a notebook, and an open mind. The lab provides the bench, equipment, and reagents; sign up on the Discord ahead of time for team formation and pre-event workshops. You can also bring your own hardware or wetware.

// Safety & the rules
Is wet-lab work at a hackathon actually safe?

Yes, when it's kept inside the envelope Biopunk is designed around: BSL-1 — well-characterized, non-pathogenic organisms (E. coli K-12, baker's yeast, standard cloning strains) on open benches. Every participant completes a safety induction before unsupervised access, the lab's safety officer supervises, and every project passes a written risk assessment at the pitch stage.

What can't I work on?

The pitch gate rules out anything human-subject (including your own samples or self-experimentation), animal work, clinical claims, gain-of-function, select agents, or anything above BSL-1. Also out for a one-week format: mammalian stable cell lines, directed evolution, and large-scale fermentation. Screened before reagents are ordered — see the biosafety gate.

Do I keep my project and IP?

Under the get-and-give ethos of this community, projects are documented openly in the gallery so the next team can learn from them — like open-source software. Specific IP terms are set by each event's organizer and any track sponsor, so read your event's rules. Rule of thumb: you keep your work and your portfolio; sponsors get visibility and recruiting access, not ownership.

// Hosts & sponsors
How do I host my own Biopunk?

Start with the host kit — the full step-by-step, from timeline and crew to budget, sponsorship, securing a lab, biosafety, judging, and demo-day run of show. Short version: partner with a community lab, lock reagent sponsors early, and work backwards from demo day. Then tell us on the Join page and we'll help connect the pieces.

How does in-kind sponsorship work?

Instead of cash, companies donate product and service access — synthetic DNA, reagents, sequencing, software, compute, or equipment time — which participants use during the lab week. It dramatically cuts the budget and gives sponsors brand exposure and recruiting access. See the roster and tiers.

Is Biopunk affiliated with iGEM or MLH?

No. Biopunk is independent, but borrows openly from both: MLH's league branding, open organizer guide, and judging science, and iGEM's scientific rigor and biosafety culture. MLH's organizer guide is Creative-Commons licensed, which is why so much of the playbook adapts so cleanly.

Where and when do events happen?

It started at Frontier Tower in San Francisco (995 Market St) and it's built to be run locally and repeatably anywhere there's a host and a willing community lab. Check the Luma calendar and Join for the founding-host network — or start one yourself.
